# AGENTS.md

**Elaine**: a Minecraft: Java Edition server core written from scratch in C++ (the name stays out of
the code, see "Naming"). The goal is **500 concurrent players on one machine**. Performance is
the product: when speed and elegance disagree, speed wins, and a claim of speed needs a number.

Code, comments, commit messages and docs are English. README.md says what the project is for and
where it is going, CONTRIBUTING.md what a contribution needs; this file is the rules of the code.

## Status (keep this section true)

README.md repeats this table for people, as goals with boxes ("Goals"): when a row changes here, tick
or add the line there.

| Area | State |
|---|---|
| Protocol | Minecraft **26.3**, protocol **777** (one version at a time, see `src/proto/gen/current.hpp`). Registries, tags, block states, items, recipes, fuel and what an explosion leaves of a block generated from the jar |
| Network | Done: io_uring reactor, framing, zlib compression, AES-CFB8, online + offline login, configuration phase, keep-alive. Known limit: encryption runs on the tick thread - 500 players in one place cost it 8-25 ms a tick in online mode |
| World | Flat generator (vanilla default layers, or the layers of an imported flat world), palette sections, biomes per 4x4x4 cell, chunk wire codec, sky + block light (port of vanilla's engine, with its loading and unloading half). **Chunks come and go**: a view's chunks are read from region files or generated by worker threads, lit and made ticking as their neighbours arrive, sent in vanilla's batches (`PlayerChunkSender`: the view is `ChunkTrackingView`'s, the pace the client's), their frames deflated by workers and cached until a change; a chunk nobody has been near for `chunk-unload-seconds` is saved and unloaded. **Saved**: an own region format (`world/region.hpp`: 512-byte sectors, checksums, a chunk is never overwritten in place) under `<level-name>/chunks/`, with the blocks, biomes and light of a chunk, its block entities, the entities standing in it and its scheduled ticks; written when a chunk leaves, every `autosave-seconds` while it stays, and at shutdown. A chunk comes back byte for byte as it left (unit and integration tests compare frames). **A vanilla world is imported** once at startup (`server/import.cpp`, current data version only): blocks, biomes, saved light, the scheduled ticks of fluids, chests, barrels and furnaces (smokers, blast furnaces) with their names, locks and what they hold; everything else of a chunk - other block entities, a chest that names a loot table, entities, the rest of its NBT - is kept as tags, unread. Compared with a vanilla 26.3 server by `tools/vanilla/world_probe.py`; the differences left are in docs/architecture.md. One dimension. **`level-type=minecraft:normal` is the overworld's terrain as far as it is ported** (`src/worldgen`, docs/architecture.md "World generation"): biomes a cell, the shape of the ground with its aquifers and the surface on it - grass over dirt, sand, snow, the bands of the badlands, icebergs, bedrock, deepslate, ore veins - and the caves and canyons of the carvers, for the same seed block for block what vanilla's `terrain` chunk status makes - and what is ported of its `features` (`feature.*`: where every feature of the overworld's biomes is placed, and of the features themselves ores so far, held to the jar's classes and to a vanilla server); no structures yet. A chunk of such a world is made in stages on the workers (`world::GenPipeline`, docs/architecture.md "Features: one order, and the workers"): features reach into the chunks around, so the chunks are decorated in one fixed order - four passes over areas of 4 x 4 chunks - that a vanilla server could have taken, and a chunk is the same whoever asks for it, in whatever order, with however many workers. What generation leaves to be done in a chunk - the water an aquifer left standing beside a cave - is done when the chunk first runs its ticks (`LevelChunk.postProcessGeneration`: `Game::post_process_chunks`), and kept with the chunk until then. Compared with a vanilla 26.3 server by `tools/vanilla/terrain_probe.py`: the chunks it saved before anything ran in them - some 350: blocks, heightmaps, what is marked to flow - and the biomes of 900. A world of terrain begins where vanilla's does (`NoiseSpawnFinder`: `spawn_origin`, held to the jar for eight seeds), and its players appear on the first ground to stand on in a spiral of chunks around that (`find_world_spawn`: `MinecraftServer.setInitialSpawn` over bare terrain - vanilla's looks at chunks that have their trees, so the block need not be the same yet). **A world has a seed** (`level-seed` when it is made, as `WorldOptions.parseSeed` reads it; vanilla's when it is imported; kept in `world.json`; clients are told its hash, `BiomeManager.obfuscateSeed`), and what a generator is made of is being ported from the jar, each part held to what the jar's own classes answer (`mcgen.py worldgen golden`, `tests/unit/worldgen_test.cpp`): the random sources (`src/worldgen/random.*`: Xoroshiro, the 48-bit one, positional factories, `WorldgenRandom`) the noises (`noise.*`: all 62 of the overworld and the three stacks of `old_blended_noise`, made from the seed; a value at a point and a volume added to a buffer, bit for bit) and the density functions (`density.*`: the 359 samplers vanilla compiles the overworld's functions into, generated from a dump of them, each as vanilla runs it at a block and over a volume - bit for bit, and with what a cache answers after which question) the biomes (`climate.*`: the tree vanilla searches the overworld's 7594 climate boxes in, and the biomes of a chunk's cells in the order vanilla asks for them - where two biomes are equally near, the one found last stays) and the first two steps of a chunk's terrain (`aquifer.*`, `terrain.*`: the density of every block and what the aquifers make of the open ones; `material.*`: the overworld's material rule, generated from the jar's 42 rule files, asked about every block of stone, with the biome of a block as `BiomeManager` zooms it ; `carver.*`: the caves and canyons that begin in the 289 chunks around, what they mark and what the aquifer makes of the marks - 92 whole chunks, each of the 56 biomes in one, against vanilla's own `doFill`, `buildSurface` and `generateCarvers`). Not there: most features and all structures (a world of terrain has its ores, and no trees, lakes or villages), an imported world going on with this generator (beyond its chunks there is flat ground or nothing), the players of an imported world, export back to Anvil, reading a world of another data version |
| Blocks | Creative only: break, place, block-change broadcast with sequence acks. Placeable: blocks whose vanilla hooks are all implemented (default state, pillar axis) and the block classes that are ported in full (below) - 770 of 1286, and water and lava, which no item places and `/setblock` and `/fill` set; the rest stay `kPlaceNone`. **docs/blocks-roadmap.md** is the checklist of all of them, generated (`tools/mcgen/roadmap.py`) |

Blocks on them: torches (plain, soul, copper; standing and wall), the redstone lamp, amethyst buds and clusters, budding amethyst, grass and mycelium (spread, die, snowy), nylium, copper that weathers (a copper chest too: the left half of a pair, and the right one with it), the lid of a chest and the `open` of a barrel, TNT's signal - plants (`VegetationBlock`: flowers, short grass and ferns, bushes, dead bushes and dry grass, azaleas, nether roots, sprouts and fungi, the cactus flower - each on what its class stands on, `state::kSupport`, and broken when that goes; petals, wildflowers and leaf litter up to four to a block) - what falls: sand, red sand, gravel, concrete powder (`server/falling.cpp`: a `FallingBlockEntity` on the entity physics, saved with its chunk) - and the blocks whose state is their shape (`server/block_shapes.cpp`): slabs (the half that is clicked; two halves are one block and drop two), stairs (the corner their neighbours make), fences, panes, bars and walls (an arm to what stands beside them; a wall tall under a block, with a post where it needs one), lanterns, chains, end rods, glazed terracotta, mushroom blocks, copper grates, and the copper of them that weathers as the shape it is. **Water and lava flow** (`world/fluid.*`, `server/fluids.cpp`: a port of `FlowingFluid` on the fluids' ticks - water a block every five ticks, a level less each block, seven blocks from a source; to the nearest way down within its reach and nowhere else; a falling column; a source between two sources, unless `water-source-conversion` - a setting here - says no; what gives way to it washed away with its drop; draining when its source goes. Lava the same at its own pace - thirty ticks a block, two levels a block, three blocks far, no new sources unless `lava-source-conversion` - and what it becomes where it meets water: obsidian or cobblestone where water comes beside it or onto it, stone where it comes down on water, basalt over soul soil beside blue ice, each with its hiss, level event 1501). **The water in a block that holds it** (`waterlogged`: set where a block is placed into a source, by `/setblock` and `/fill`, or where a source would form in a dry one) is a source where the block stands: ticked when the block's shape is asked, out through what the block's shape leaves open, left behind as water when the block goes. **The sponge** takes up the water around it - sixty-four cells, six steps far - when it is placed and when water comes beside it. **Buckets** (`server/buckets.cpp`: `BucketItem.use`, by `use_item`) pour water and lava out where their holder looks and take them up again - the look is `BlockGetter.clip` (`world::clip`: the outline of every block state, moved where vanilla moves it, and the fluid if it is asked for); a creative player's bucket stays what it is. Compared with vanilla by `tools/vanilla/swing_probe.py`. Compared with a vanilla 26.3 server (`tools/vanilla/block_probe.py`: which block breaks first, how many ticks a lamp and a fall take, what random ticks leave, which state every shape takes, which cell of water and of lava becomes what in which tick); the differences left are in docs/architecture.md. Not there: bubble columns, fire (lava lights nothing), water in the blocks of an imported world whose class is not ported, weather (no rain or snow on blocks), ice melting, comparators, game rules, scheduled block ticks of an imported world (kept as tags; its fluid ticks are read), anything that grows (bone meal, saplings, crops, mushrooms), plants of two blocks, flower pots, candles |
| Block entities, menus | **Chest** (single and double, the lid, two players in one chest, spills when broken; of copper too - eight of them, which pair across ages and waxes as the less weathered block, and keep what they hold as they weather), **barrel**, **furnace**, **smoker** and **blast furnace** (one block entity with three sets of recipes: fuel, the `lit` state, the menu's four numbers, twice the speed on half the fuel in the two fast ones), **crafting table** and the inventory's own grid (shaped, shapeless and transmute recipes: 1235 of 1266), **TNT** (flint and steel, a block of redstone; explosions take blocks, drop what their loot tables give, set off other TNT, push players, throw items and mobs about, break armor stands). A container's name is its menu's title and its lock is honoured (`CustomName`, `lock`: an item predicate against what is in the hand); it is loaded from a tag as vanilla loads it (`Game::load_block_entity`: `/setblock chest{...}`, `/fill`, a block item that carries a name, a `container` or `block_entity_data`). **Signs** (`server/signs.cpp`): standing, on a wall, hanging from a ceiling and between walls, of every wood - turned to whoever places them, written on (the editor that opens, the one player whose lines are taken), dyed, made to glow, waxed; what a client is shown of one is its update tag, which lies with its chunk (the chunk's packet carries it, `block_entity_data` follows a change). `server::Menu` is `AbstractContainerMenu` for all of them. Compared with a vanilla 26.3 server (`tools/vanilla/container_probe.py`): menus byte for byte; the differences left are in docs/architecture.md. Not there: experience (no orbs), the recipe book, the 31 recipes that compute their result from components, comparators, loot tables of containers (a chest that names one is not placed), wax and scraping (honeycomb, axes), the trapped and the ender chest |
| Entities | One SoA table for everything (`entity::Store`: players are the first rows, slot + generation handles, network id = slot + 1). Tracker (`entity::Tracker`) is a port of vanilla's `ChunkMap.TrackedEntity` + `ServerEntity`: who sees whom, add/remove bundles, movement deltas, rotation, head, velocity, entity data - encoded once per entity, copied per viewer. What a tag says of an entity and few are told - a name and whether it shows, tags, whether it is heard, glows, thinks (`NoAI`), its health, an armor stand's pose and what it wears - is cold (`entity::Cold`, by slot): shown to whoever sees it, written into what it saves as, kept with its chunk. Compared with a vanilla 26.3 server (`command_probe.py`, section eggs). What an egg makes is what `/summon` makes: no `Mob.finalizeSpawn` (variant, equipment, size, left hand), no sound as it appears. Not there: an egg used at water or into the air (`SpawnEggItem.use`: not ported yet), spawners, a baby of an egg used on its mob, a difficulty (monsters come as on any but peaceful) |
| Physics | `src/phys`: a port of `Entity.move` and what is around it (collision with every block shape, stepping up, the block stood on, bounces, cobwebs, honey, slime), of `ItemEntity.tick`, `PrimedTnt.tick` and of `LivingEntity.travel` with nobody steering. Items, primed TNT, falling blocks, the armor stand and 59 kinds of mob move by it; living things push each other and are pushed by players. **In water and lava** (`Entity.updateFluidInteraction`, 26.x's `EntityFluidInteraction`): an item floats up, what lives sinks slowly and walks on the bottom, a current carries both (`FlowingFluid.getFlow`: `world::fluid_flow`), a fall ends in water; the axolotl, the frog and the nautilus by their own `travelInWater`, and what a mob's `MoveControl` adds whatever the mob wants (a frog floats up, a drowned sinks). **Fire**: lava lights what is in it, water puts it out, the flag is shown and `Fire` is kept; an item has its five points of health and burns up (but one of netherite), nothing else takes damage. **Bit for bit** what a vanilla 26.3 server computes: `tools/vanilla/physics_probe.py` steps one tick by tick through 417 scenarios (341, 16 of falling blocks and 60 of what is in a fluid or on fire, each lot in a recording of its own) and records every position and velocity as exact doubles - and `Fire`, and an item's health - `tests/unit/physics_test.cpp` replays them. What lies still is not ticked in full (`phys::item_rests`, `Game::sleep` / `wake`): the same bits, 4 ns a tick for an item instead of 135, nothing for a mob. Not there: a brain (a mob falls, slides, sinks and is pushed, no more - vanilla's float in water, by a goal; what flies, swims, climbs or walks on lava stays where it is put, and so does the turtle), bubble columns, damage to what lives (fall, fire, lava, drowning), a player's splash and the sound of swimming (the splash of everything else, the hiss of a fire going out and an item burning up are heard), what a player does to its own velocity, entities that collide (boats, shulkers), pistons |
| Players | Join/leave, tab list. **Kept**: where a player stood and looked, whether it flew, its inventory and the slot in hand - one file a player under `<level-name>/playerstate/`, read by a worker before the player joins, written when it leaves and every `autosave-seconds` (`server/player_data.*`, `server/files.*`); what was on its cursor or in a crafting grid is dropped as it leaves, as in vanilla. Item entities: thrown the way the player looks, falling, sliding, merged, picked up, expired. Compared with a vanilla 26.3 server (`tools/vanilla/inventory_probe.py`); the differences left are in docs/architecture.md. Not there: component hashes of clicks, what a bundle does when it is clicked, the behaviour components (food, tool, equippable...) |

What a command answers was compared with a vanilla 26.3 server, text components byte for byte (`tools/vanilla/command_probe.py`); the differences left are in docs/architecture.md. Not there: components in an item predicate, `data merge` / `modify` / `remove` and a storage anything is written to, `tp ... facing`, the selector options `scores` / `advancements` / `predicate`, killing a player, and every command whose subject does not exist yet (weather, game modes, effects, scoreboards, `execute`, `data`...) |
| Time | 26.x's world clocks (`server/clock.*`: a port of `ServerClockManager`, time markers, the day's sky-light track; tables generated from the jar's timelines). | Chat | What a player types goes to everyone who shows chat as vanilla sends the message of a player without a chat session (`server/chat.cpp`): `player_chat`, unsigned, counted per recipient - the rest of the packet is encoded once and copied behind each count in a frame that is never deflated. `disguised_chat` for what the console says and what a command says for a player; "joined the game" / "left the game"; chat visibility; the limit on messages in a row; a client that is out of step with what it was sent is put out. | NBT and text | `nbt::Doc` reads a tag as vanilla's loader does (depth, quota, modified UTF-8, wrapped list elements) into a tape over the bytes - no tree; `nbt::Writer` builds one; `Doc::write` puts a loaded tag back in one canonical form. **SNBT** (`nbt/snbt.*`): the reader is a port of `TagParser` and its grammar rule by rule - a wrong tag gets vanilla's error at vanilla's place - the printers are `Tag.toString()` and, as text, `NbtUtils.toPrettyComponent` (`text::pretty_nbt`, a compound's entries in the order of vanilla's HashMap); `nbt/ops.*` compares two tags (`NbtUtils.compareNbt`), merges them and follows an NBT path; all of it compared with a vanilla 26.3 server through `/data get`, `/tellraw` and `nbt=` (`tools/vanilla/command_probe.py`). `text::transcode` is `ComponentSerialization.CODEC` both ways: any spelling of a text component in, the bytes vanilla writes for it out (entry order of its HashMap included) - compared with a vanilla 26.3 server over some 220 spellings (`tools/vanilla/component_probe.py`). On items: custom name, item name, lore, books, sign text; custom data and (block) entity data. Not read (the stack is refused): score / selector / nbt / object contents, a `show_item` whose stack has components, `show_dialog` / `custom`; The server's own texts are built with `text::Text` (what commands answer, a kick's reason) and leave through the same codec; the console prints them in English (`text::lang`: en_us from the jar). ## Commands

```sh
cmake --preset release && cmake --build --preset release   # -O3, x86-64-v3, LTO  -> build/release/elaine
cmake --build --preset release --target server_exe         # while iterating: only what is about to be run - server_exe
                                                           # (elaine), unit_tests or bench, several may be named. Each is
                                                           # an LTO link of the whole program: 10 s alone, 40 s when all
                                                           # three run at once, which is what a build without --target does
ctest --preset release                                     # unit tests + integration tests; wants server_exe and unit_tests
tools/check.py -k chest --unit 'Container*'                # build what these need, run them, print a line a step and only
                                                           # what failed; --failed: the scenarios that failed last time;
                                                           # `tools/check.py full`: everything "done" asks for (the verify skill)
cmake --preset dev && cmake --build --preset dev           # Debug + ASan/UBSan + DCHECKs; --target server_exe is all the
                                                           # line below needs: no tests, no benchmarks, a third fewer files
ASAN_OPTIONS=detect_leaks=1 python3 tests/integration/run.py build/dev/elaine   # must be clean before "done"

build/release/unit_tests --gtest_filter='Cfb8*'            # one unit suite
python3 tests/integration/run.py build/release/elaine -k join   # one scenario; --slow adds the timer tests (15 s .. 5 min);
                                                           # -j 0: sixteen side by side, the longest first (all of them:
                                                           # 40 s, not five minutes); several binaries are one pool
python3 tests/integration/run.py build/release/elaine -k crowd_500 --slow   # 500 bots in sight of each other: tick time, bytes
build/release/bench [filter]                               # micro-benchmarks (release/native builds only); `bench tracker` = entities,
                                                           # `bench item` / `bench menu` / `bench pickup` = items, `bench nbt` / `bench text`,
                                                           # `bench craft` / `bench furnace` / `bench collide` / `bench clip` / `bench blast` = blocks,
                                                           # `bench physics` = an entity's tick, by what it is doing (ns per entity),
                                                           # `bench command` = reading a command line, encoding its answer
build/release/elaine --online-mode=false --server-port=25565   # run; any server.properties key works as --key=value
                                                           # (`level-seed`, `level-type` - minecraft:flat, the default here,
                                                           # or minecraft:normal - say what a world that is made new is)
                                                           # (and `random-tick-speed`, `advance-time`, `water-source-conversion`,
                                                           # `lava-source-conversion` and `tick-rate`: game rules, and what
                                                           # `/tick rate` begins at, in vanilla)
                                                           # (`generation-cache-mb`: how much of what the generator made beyond
                                                           # what was asked for is kept for the chunks beside it)
                                                           # its stdin is the console: `op <name>` there makes the first operator
                                                           # the world is kept in ./world (--level-name=<dir>; --level-name= keeps
                                                           # nothing): a directory with a vanilla world in it is imported first

./install.sh                                               # a fresh clone, once: packages, the jar, the generated tables, a release
                                                           # build (it runs Mojang's code - see "Protocol data")
python3 tools/mcgen/mcgen.py --help                        # protocol data pipeline
python3 tools/mcgen/mcgen.py setup                         # only the tables: whatever of fetch / reports / blockdata / gamedata /
                                                           # worldgen is not done yet, then generate, then compare with the lock
python3 tools/mcgen/mcgen.py data blocks '^minecraft:oak_stairs$'   # a fact that only exists at runtime (shapes, hooks,
                                                           # fields, attributes, enums, menus): see the mc-reference skill
python3 tools/mcgen/roadmap.py                             # rewrite docs/blocks-roadmap.md after porting a block class (--check: is it stale?)
```

Other presets: `native` (`-march=native`, for the production box), `profile` (frame pointers + `-g`, for `perf`).

## Layout

```
src/base/     types.hpp (u8..u64, LIKELY, DCHECK), buf.hpp (ByteBuf, BufPool), spsc.hpp (lock-free queues), log, clock,
              java_number.* (a float as Java prints it),
              workers.* (worker threads: a task out through one SPSC ring, its result back through another; the owner may
              sleep until one is done)
```

## Architecture rules (details and reasons: docs/architecture.md)

Threads: **reactor threads** (`net-N`, one io_uring each) own sockets; the **tick thread** owns all game
state; **workers** (`work-N`, `base::Workers`) do what would block or hold up a tick - reading and writing
region files, inflating, deflating, generating. They share nothing mutable.

1. **Threads exchange data only through SPSC rings** (`base/spsc.hpp`). No mutex, no atomics-as-locks,
   no shared containers on any per-packet or per-tick path. Mutexes exist only on cold paths
   (`Reactor::run_on_loop`, the auth worker queue).
2. **One writer per outbound stream.** Before `promote_to_play` the reactor thread writes a connection's
   output; after it only the tick thread does (the cipher state travels in the Join record).
3. **Inbound play packets** are decrypted, split and inflated on the reactor thread, then copied once into
   the reactor's `SpscBytes` ring; the tick thread drains every ring at the start of each tick.
4. **Outbound**: packets are appended to one `ByteBuf` per player per tick; `Game::flush` encrypts all of
   them in one batch (8 streams interleaved) and hands each buffer to the reactor — one send per player
   per tick.
5. **Serialize once.** Anything sent to more than one player is encoded/framed/compressed once and
   `memcpy`'d (see `Game::broadcast`, `Dimension::frame`, the config burst in `static_data`, and the
   entity tracker, where one entity's update is one byte range that every viewer copies).
6. **Connection slots are `(slot, generation)` handles**, never pointers across threads. A slot is reused
   only after the tick thread sends `Release`.
7. **Vanilla on the wire.** The server must be indistinguishable from vanilla to an unmodified client.
   Behaviour that the client can observe is copied from the vanilla jar, not invented.
8. **Every block change goes through `Game::set_block`** (never `world_.set_block` from game code): it is
   what removes and creates block entities, spills their contents, and tells the neighbours. Its flags are
   vanilla's (`kUpdate*`): a command that sets blocks passes what `SetBlockCommand` passes. A block that breaks
   by itself goes through `break_block` (`Level.destroyBlock`), and nothing calls another block's hook
   directly: what a change sets off is queued in vanilla's order (`add_update`), never run where it is made.
9. **A block is placeable only when its class is ported** - by hand or by `/setblock`, which refuses the rest. Porting one: read every hook the class chain
   overrides (`mcgen.py data blocks <name> --field hooks`), implement them, enter the class with that hook
   set in `PORTED_BLOCKS` (`tools/mcgen/gen_game.py`), `mcgen.py generate`, `roadmap.py`. The generator
   refuses a placeable block whose loot table, signal or explosion hooks it cannot turn into tables.
10. **A worker is handed a whole thing and hands a whole thing back** (`base::Task`: `work` on the worker, `done`
    on the tick thread): bytes to deflate, a file descriptor and where to read, a chunk outside the world
    (`world::LoadedChunk`, in an arena of its own), the sixteen chunks of an area that is decorated
    (`world::GenChunk`s, which are in one place at a time) - never the world, a pool, or anything the tick thread goes
    on using. What the files hold is known to the tick thread alone (`world::ChunkStore`); one write per region
    at a time owns that region's `RegionFile`.
11. **A chunk enters and leaves the world through `world::Dimension`** (`request` / `pump`, `save`, `unload`; `forget`
    for one that was asked for and is wanted no more), and
    the game is told (`adopted()`, `ready()`, `stopped()`): what it keeps per chunk - block entities, entities -
    is written and read in `server/persist.cpp` and nowhere else. Whatever makes a chunk differ from its bytes on
    disk marks it (`Chunk::dirty`, `Dimension::mark_dirty`): a chunk that is not marked is not written again.
    Chunks arrive in the order they were asked for, whichever worker was done first.
12. **A generated chunk is a function of the seed and its place** - not of who asked, when, in which order, or how
    many workers there are. The order features are placed in is `world::GenPipeline`'s and is fixed (four passes over
    areas of 4 x 4 chunks); everything it holds is a cache that may be thrown away and made again, the same to the
    bit. Nothing a generator does may read the world, the clock, or a chunk it was not handed.

## Performance rules

- **Hot path = anything per packet, per entity, per block or per tick.** There: no heap allocation, no
  locks, no exceptions, no virtual calls, no `std::function`, `std::shared_ptr`, `std::string` building,
  iostreams, `std::unordered_map`/`std::map`, or logging. Cold paths (login, config load, errors) may
  use whatever is clearest.
- **That list is a default, not a goal.** If a `bench` number (release build, same machine) shows that a
  listed construct is faster for a given case, use it and put the measurement in a comment. Count the cost
  per item, not per call: one indirect call or one amortized buffer growth per batch is fine, one per
  entity or per packet is not. A lookup by key on a hot path wants a flat open-addressing table, not
  `std::unordered_map` (a heap node and a cache miss per element). Exceptions stay out regardless - the
  build has them off.
- **Data layout follows the access pattern.** Data iterated in bulk is SoA: parallel arrays indexed by a
  dense index, removal by swap-with-last (see `entity::Store`). Data touched one object at a time
  (a `Conn` on a completion, the tracker's `Sent` per entity) stays a struct, hot fields first. Do not
  SoA things that are never iterated.
- **Bytes on the wire are the unit of cost.** In online mode every outbound byte is one AES block
  (CFB8), ~3 ns - several times what encoding and copying it cost. A byte saved in a packet that goes to
  N viewers is saved N times: small entity ids, one-byte frame lengths for tiny packets (`frame_begin`).
- **Encoders write through raw pointers after one capacity check**: `u8* p = buf.reserve(bound)` →
  `put_*` → `buf.commit(p)`. Every packet type exposes `bound()`. `ByteBuf` keeps 64 bytes of slack so
  writers may use wide stores.
- **Bit packing**: keep palettes/bitfields in the smallest width that the wire or the cache allows, pack
  with shifts/`pdep`/`pext`, never one value per byte "for simplicity". Wire layouts are fixed by vanilla;
  in-memory layouts are ours to make dense.
- **SIMD**: the baseline is `x86-64-v3` (AVX2, BMI1/2, FMA, LZCNT, MOVBE) plus AES-NI/PCLMUL, so
  intrinsics up to AVX2 need no guard. Wider code goes under `#if defined(__AVX512F__)` /
  `__VAES__` so a `native` build picks it up; always keep the AVX2 path. No runtime dispatch.
  Give SIMD loops a compile-time trip count — a variable count spills the vectors to the stack
  (this cost 35% in `cfb8_encrypt_batch`; see the `perf` skill).
- **No `-ffast-math`**: physics must reproduce vanilla's IEEE results.
- **Built with `-fno-exceptions -fno-rtti`.** Errors are return values or a sticky flag (`proto::Reader::ok`).
  A malformed packet closes the connection; it never throws and never aborts the server.
- **Prove it**: a performance change comes with before/after numbers from `bench` (or `perf`), same
  machine, release build. Add a benchmark when you add a hot path.

## Protocol data — the vanilla jar is the only source of truth

Never write a packet layout, id, or registry value from memory or from a wiki: 26.x differs from 1.21.x in
places nobody documents (examples already hit: `login_finished` gained a session UUID, play `login`
gained `onlineMode` and encodes game modes as VarInts, chunk sections gained a fluid count,
`accept_teleportation` echoes the position, light masks are `BitSet` byte arrays instead of long arrays). Since 26.1 Mojang ships unobfuscated jars, so the answer is
always one command away — use the `mc-reference` skill.

`tools/mcgen/mcgen.py` downloads the official jars (sha1-checked) into `.cache/` and generates
`src/proto/gen/v<protocol>/`. Steps that only read the jar (`fetch`, `find`, `javap`, `codec`, `packets`,
`pseudo`, `data`, `generate`, `check`) are safe to run freely. `generate` leaves a file whose content did
not change as it was and names the ones it rewrote, so the build after it recompiles only what includes those.

**The generated directory is not in git; its lock is.** The tables are Mojang's data, so every clone makes
them from the jar (`./install.sh`, or `mcgen.py setup`), and `src/proto/gen/v<protocol>.sha256` says what
they must be. `generate` rewrites the lock - when it changes, commit it with the change of the generator that
moved it (`PORTED_BLOCKS`, a new table) - and every build begins with `mcgen.py check`: tables that are not
what the lock says stop it (after a pull: `mcgen.py setup`). Never commit anything under `src/proto/gen/v*/`,
the jars, or anything else from `.cache/`.

**`mcgen.py reports` executes Mojang's data generator (the downloaded server jar, needs Java 25).
Ask the user before running it**, and likewise `blockdata`, `gamedata` and `worldgen` — these four are the mcgen
steps that run downloaded code — and `setup` and `./install.sh`, which run them (each only when its
output is missing or older than the jar and the extractor: `.cache/mc/<version>/stamps/`). `reports` produces `registries.json` and `blocks.json`, without which
there are no static-registry ids: no tags, no block ids. Never substitute ids from memory, guesses or
third-party mirrors.

`mcgen.py blockdata` (`tools/mcgen/extract/Extract.java`) writes what the per-state tables are made of: light,
fluids, occlusion, and every collision shape twice - as boxes, and as the grid of cells vanilla holds it as
(`collision_grids`: an entity collides with the cells and steps up to the grid's heights, so the grid is not
rebuilt from the boxes).

`mcgen.py gamedata` (`tools/mcgen/extract/GameData.java`) loads the game as a server does before it
creates a world - no world, no server, no network - and writes everything else that exists only at
runtime into `.cache/mc/<version>/gamedata/`: every shape and predicate per block state, which class
overrides which hook, what each block/item/entity type was constructed with, default attributes,
synchronized entity data, all static registries, enums, static tables, menu slots. Read it with
`mcgen.py data` before porting a block, an item, a mob or a menu. It is a reference so far: generated
C++ takes from it what `gen.py` / `gen_game.py` have been taught to, table by table - so far: block classes
and the hooks they override, block entity types, loot table names, speed and bounce factors, crafting
remainders, which states conduct redstone - and `generate` stops without it.

`mcgen.py worldgen` (`tools/mcgen/extract/WorldgenRef.java`) loads the game the same way and writes what
world generation is once vanilla has built it, into `.cache/mc/<version>/worldgen/`: `program.json`, the
overworld's density functions as vanilla *compiled* them (`RandomState.getSampler`: a graph of sampler
objects, field by field - a division by a constant is a multiplication there, a `min` whose ranges do not
overlap is gone, and that is what decides the bits), and `climate.json`, the biome parameters with the
R-tree they are searched by. It compiles for two seeds and stops if anything but a noise's permutation and
offsets differs. And `math.bin` (`WorldgenMath.java`): the JVM asked for `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` of every angle a
feature can make of a float, and where its answer is not the one `src/worldgen/jmath.cpp` computes. The JSON under `data/minecraft/worldgen/` in the jar says what a world is made of; these
files say what vanilla made of it, and the tables of `src/worldgen` are generated from both.

The same extractor decorates chunks (`tools/mcgen/extract/WorldgenFeatures.java`): vanilla's `features` status
run without a server - the real `WorldGenRegion` over proto chunks made by the steps above, on a level, a
chunk source and a server that are made without their constructors and answer only what a region and the
features ask. `mcgen.py worldgen census` decorates chunks of every biome and counts what a generator needs
a rule for (reads beyond the nine chunks a decoration may write to, refused writes, questions about light);
`mcgen.py worldgen replay` decorates in an order it is given (`tools/vanilla/feature_probe.py` writes it).

## Lua plugins (planned — design for it now)

Plugins will run on **LuaJIT**, on the tick thread only. Constraints this puts on today's code:

- Game state that plugins will read must be reachable as plain C data: POD structs and SoA arrays behind
  an `extern "C"` API, so LuaJIT FFI can read a column directly instead of making a C call per entity
  (`entity::Columns` is that view of the entity table).
- Entities, players and connections are referred to by generation-checked integer handles, never
  pointers that could dangle inside a script (`entity::Handle`: slot + generation in 53 bits, so it
  survives being a Lua number).
- Hooks must cost nothing when unused: one bit test per event type before any dispatch.
- Never call into Lua from a reactor or worker thread, and never let a Lua error unwind through C++
  frames (we compile without exceptions): every entry goes through `lua_pcall`.
- A plugin's command is registered like the server's own: `Game::commands().add(syntax, level, handler,
  user)` with a C function and a pointer (`server/command.hpp`). The handler reads its arguments by name
  from a `cmd::Invocation` - numbers and strings as they are, entities as handles - so the Lua binding is
  one trampoline; nothing in the tree, the parsers or the `commands` packet knows who registered a node.

- The plugin interface is a legal boundary as well as a technical one: `PLUGIN-EXCEPTION` lets a plugin carry
  any licence as long as it touches the server only through that interface. So the interface is one
  documented thing - the FFI declarations a plugin needs are handed to it by the server, not copied out of
  `src/` - and nothing else of the server is reachable from a script.

Java plugin (Bukkit/Paper) compatibility is explicitly out of scope for now.

## Testing rules

- Every packet layout gets a unit test that decodes field by field (`tests/unit/wire_test.cpp` pattern) and,
  if it is part of a flow, an assertion in `tests/integration/run.py`.
- `tests/integration/mcproto.py` is an independent implementation (own framing, zlib, OpenSSL via
  ctypes). Keep it independent: never import server code or copy constants other than the generated ids.
  Its `Client` keeps a model of the entities it was shown (`c.entities`) and of its own menu (`c.slots`,
  `c.cursor`), written from the vanilla client's handlers; scenarios assert on that model, and `settle()`
  waits whole server ticks. `server_menu()` in run.py makes the server send its whole menu (a click with
  a stale state id): the way to see what the server holds rather than what the client believes.
- Every server a scenario starts has a world of its own (`Server` in run.py: a temporary `level-name`, removed
  when it stops; `world=` runs a directory that is kept, for scenarios that stop a server and start it again) -
  and no random ticks (`random-tick-speed=0`) unless the scenario asks for them (`@test(random_tick_speed=4096)`):
  grass under a block that was placed dies whenever chance has it, and what a scenario is told must not depend on when.
  Its server ticks five times as fast as vanilla's (`tick-rate=100`, `TICK_RATE` in run.py): a scenario counts
  ticks, not seconds, so that is a fifth of the waiting. One that measures ticks or times something by the clock
  asks for vanilla's rate (`@test(tick_rate=20)`: the crowds, `heavy_stacks`), and a window in seconds is a number
  of ticks times `srv.tick`. What a worker does takes its own time, not ticks: a scenario waits for it (the chunks
  of a view: `sync_chunks`), never a number of ticks.
  A world that comes back is compared as frames: the chunks one client was sent before, another after, byte
  for byte (`frames()`). A vanilla world for the import is written out by hand (`anvil_world()` in run.py, from
  the jar's layouts), as NBT is.
- NBT in tests is written out by hand, never by the code under test: `tests/unit/nbt_bytes.hpp` (C++) builds
  tags from the layouts in the jar, `mc.nbt()` / `Reader.nbt()` in mcproto.py are the test client's own
  reader and writer (`mc.snbt()` prints a value; a dict keeps the wire's entry order, so `mc.nbt(read) ==
  mc.nbt(expected)` compares bytes). What a *codec* makes of a tag is not a layout and cannot be checked by
  reading alone: extend `tools/vanilla/component_probe.py` and compare with the vanilla server.
- The integration tests prove framing, crypto, compression and sequencing. They do **not** prove that a
  layout matches the vanilla client — that comes from reading the jar. Say "verified against the jar"
  or "not verified" in your summary; a real-client check is the user's to run.
- Scenarios run side by side (`run.py -j`, which ctest and `tools/check.py` use), each in a process of its own:
  a scenario shares nothing with another - no fixed port, no file outside its own world - and waits for events
  (`settle()`, `wait_log()`), not for an amount of time. One that measures the server's ticks with every core
  to itself (the crowds of `--slow`) is `@test(alone=True)` and runs after the rest. A client joins with the smallest view (`join()`'s
  default, 2): chunks are sent at the pace of ticks, and a scenario that is about the view asks for one.
- Before calling work done: `ctest --preset release` passes **and** the integration suite is clean under
  the `dev` preset (ASan/UBSan/LSan) - `tools/check.py full` runs both. The server must exit with code 0 on SIGTERM.

## Conventions

- **Naming**: the project's name appears only in `CMakeLists.txt` (`project`, `MC_EXE`) and docs. Namespaces are
  layers (`base`, `proto`, `nbt`, `text`, `crypto`, `net`, `item`, `phys`, `worldgen`, `entity`, `world`, `server`), macros are unprefixed or `MC_`/`PROTO_`. Do not
  introduce the project's name into identifiers.
- Types `snake_case` for functions/variables, `PascalCase` for types, `kConstant`, members end in `_`.
  Fixed-width ints are `u8…u64`, `i8…i64`, `usize`.
- Comments explain *why* (the constraint, the vanilla behaviour being matched, the measured reason for an
  optimization). No comments restating code.
- **Commands**: one `Registry::add` line per way of writing the command, in the words and the order of
  vanilla's class in `net.minecraft.server.commands` (the client reads the tree too); the handler is a
  static function of `Game::Commands` (`server/commands.cpp`) and answers only through `Invocation::success`
  / `fail` with `Text::translatable(<vanilla's key>)`. An argument type vanilla has and this server lacks is
  a parser in `command_args.cpp` written from its class, error keys and cursors included. What a command
  cannot do yet is refused in words of our own, never half done. Extend `tools/vanilla/command_probe.py`
  with the new command's cases and compare with the vanilla server before it is called done.
- **Chat**: whatever reaches a client's chat leaves through `server/chat.cpp`. A player's words go out by
  `broadcast_chat` / `send_chat`, and nothing else sends `player_chat`: its first field is a count per recipient
  that the client checks, kept in `deliver_chat`. The server's own words go out by `system_message` /
  `broadcast_system`, which ask what the recipient shows (`ChatVisiblity`). A command that says something takes
  a `message` argument and `Commands::chat_message`, which decides whose words they are. New cases go into the
  talking part of `tools/vanilla/command_probe.py` before they are called done.
- **Time**: a clock is changed through `Game::clock_changed` (everyone is told at once, and what is computed
  from the time is computed again), and nothing reads "the time of day" but through a clock and a timeline
  (`server/clock.hpp`): there is no day-time field to add one to. `clock.cpp` keeps vanilla's float expressions
  as they are - a client computes the same clocks by itself. A new command argument whose suggestions the
  server gives (`ask_server`) is answered in `Game::ask_server`, and its lines go into `command_probe.py`.
- **Physics**: `src/phys` is a port, and it is right when it gives vanilla's bits - not when it looks right. Keep
  vanilla's expressions as they are written (`v + 0.0`, two multiplications where one would do, float where vanilla
  has float); a shortcut must give the same answer as the long way and is tested against it (`world::slow::`, the
  lockstep test of what is at rest). New behaviour - a block that acts on entities, a fluid, another kind of mover -
  gets scenarios in `physics_probe.py`, a new recording (`record`, then `emit` into `tests/unit/data/`), and passes
  `Physics.ReplaysWhatAVanillaServerRecorded` before it is called done. Whatever changes what an entity at rest would
  read wakes it (`Game::wake`, `wake_near`, `wake_touching`): a tick that is skipped must be one that changes nothing.
  A fixture of fluid in `physics_probe.py` is the fluid as it stands when it has run out (the game ticks while it is
  recorded; `record` stops if a block of such a plot changes), and who is recorded in it is what stands still on a
  vanilla server with its speed set to nothing: a mob with a `FloatGoal` only where water is shallow, and a kind
  that cannot be recorded at all is not moved (`OWN_MOVEMENT` in gen_physics.py), never moved by guess. How a type
  moves in a fluid is a row of `gen::physics::kEntity`, read off its classes; the generator stops at a class that
  declares a fluid method it has no word for. `phys` says what a tick did to an entity's fire and what lava and fire
  took of it; whose health that is, is the game's business (`Game::took_fire`: an item's).
- **World generation**: `src/worldgen` is a port of 26.3's generator, and it is right when a chunk is vanilla's for the
  same seed, block for block. Never from memory, and not from what is known of 1.18 - 1.21: 26.x rewrote it. The density
  functions are run from what vanilla *compiled* (`mcgen.py worldgen` dumps the samplers; `gen_worldgen.py` makes the
  program of them), not from the data pack's JSON: a sampler class that is new in the dump is ported in `density.cpp` -
  both `value` and `volume`, which are two methods in vanilla and not the same bits - and entered in `SAMPLERS`. Keep
  vanilla's expressions, in float where it has float, and its *order of questions*: a context's caches answer by what
  they were asked before, a biome search by the search before it, an aquifer by which cell was asked first. A random
  draw is of the source vanilla has at that place (`random.hpp`: separate types, no virtual call). The material rule
  is run from tables of the jar's rule files (`MaterialTables` in gen_worldgen.py: a named rule compiled wherever it is
  named, a type that is new stops the generator); what a rule or a condition means is a case in `material.cpp`, written
  from its class - with what the class remembers for a column or a block, and nothing more. A draw is a statement of
  its own wherever two stand in one expression (`carver.cpp`): Java evaluates left to right, C++ as it likes. New behaviour gets
  a script in `WorldgenGolden.java` - the jar's own classes asked, their answers written down, floats as bits; a whole
  chunk step by step through `ChunkMaker` - `mcgen.py worldgen golden --emit`, and a test in
  `tests/unit/worldgen_test.cpp` that replays it, and is then held to a vanilla server
  (`tools/vanilla/compare.py terrain_probe`: what vanilla saved of chunks nothing has run in), before it is called done. A generator is called by several workers
  at once: what a call works with is its thread's (`generator.cpp`), and a chunk's terrain reads nothing of another chunk.
  What reaches from one chunk into another - features - is done in `ChunkGenerator::decorate`, an area of sixteen chunks a
  task, in the order of `world/gen_pipeline.hpp`; a change there is held to `tests/unit/gen_pipeline_test.cpp` (the same
  chunks the long way, by any order of requests, with what is kept thrown away at random).
  **Features** (`feature.*`, `region.*`): what a biome puts into a chunk is tables (`tools/mcgen/gen_features.py`, from the jar's
  `placed_feature/` and `feature/` and from `features.json` - the one order vanilla sorts all biomes' features into, which seeds
  each feature's random source); what a kind of placement or a type of feature *does* is a function written from its class.
  A type is ported by its function in `feature.cpp`, its name in `PORTED_FEATURES` (gen_features.py; a type in neither that
  list nor `UNPORTED_FEATURES` stops the generator) and its table; what is made of an unported type is left out of a
  decoration (`Placed::ported`), and nothing else moves by that. A predicate that asks only which block state is at a place - a
  tag, blocks, a fluid, "solid", a sturdy face - is a set of block states made by the generator, never a list in code; the
  sets are held to vanilla's own predicates over every state (`WorldgenFeature.StateSetsAreVanillasPredicates`). Keep
  `FeaturePlacer`'s order - a placement makes all its positions, with all their draws, before the first goes on - and every
  draw a statement of its own. Code reads and writes the world through `worldgen::Region` only: its rules of the edge are about
  the nine chunks around the one decorated, so a chunk comes out the same in any rectangle. `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` are
  `jmath::sin` and `jmath::cos`, never the C library's (HotSpot's last bit is its own): they are the JVM's for the arguments
  it was asked about, so a new formula that makes an angle of a float is a domain in `WorldgenMath.java` first (its sine in
  C++ and in Java are one algorithm: change both or neither). New behaviour gets its records in
  `WorldgenFeatures.golden` (`mcgen.py worldgen golden --emit`, `tests/unit/feature_test.cpp`: a kind of feature in
  `KINDS_OF_FEATURE` is decorated alone and compared feature by feature) and is held to a vanilla server by
  `tools/vanilla/compare.py feature_probe` before it is called done.
- **Block updates**: a block's reaction to the world is a case in the hook functions of `server/block_updates.cpp`
  (`neighbor_changed`, `update_shape`, `on_place`, `tick_block`, `random_tick`, `can_survive`), written from the method of
  its class and named after it; which states have which hook is generated (`state::kHooks`). The orders are vanilla's
  and stay: neighbours are told W E D U N S, shapes are asked W E N S D U, ticks run by (time, priority, order of
  scheduling). A block with a `randomTick` is entered in `RANDOM_TICK_KINDS` (gen_game.py) - and in `RANDOM_TICK_ALWAYS`
  unless `world::Dimension` can count when its tick has something to do (as it does for what spreads and what dies):
  a section that is counted as dormant is not ticked, and the `dev` build stops when one changes a block. A block
  whose state is its shape answers in `server/block_shapes.cpp` (`shaped_for_placement`, `shaped_update`): its
  properties are one layout for the whole family (`FAMILY_LAYOUTS` in gen_game.py, checked for every block of it),
  and what it asks of the blocks around it is a bit a state (`state::kConnect`, `state::kWallAbove`: tags, classes
  and shapes, made into tables by gen.py) - never a list of blocks in code. A class whose `updateShape` only asks
  for the tick of the water in it is entered in `NO_SHAPE_HOOK` (its dry states have no shape hook; the tick of a wet block's
  water is asked for in `Game::update_shape`, for every class at once). A plant (`VegetationBlock`) is entered in
  `PLANT_SUPPORT` with the rule of what it stands on - a tag, mostly - which gen.py makes a bit a state
  (`state::kSupport`); the block names its bit, the code names no rule. New behaviour gets its steps in
  `tools/vanilla/block_probe.py` and is compared with the vanilla server before it is called done.
- **Fluids**: a fluid is what a block state holds (`world::fluid_of`: the id of its `FluidState`, tables `fluid::k*`), never a
  block that is asked for by name; a cell of it is the fluid's own block (`world::fluid_block`), and a flow changes blocks through
  `Game::set_block` like everything else - the next cell asks for its tick because it heard of the change (`LiquidBlock`'s hooks,
  kind `kLiquid`). What a fluid asks of the world before it moves is a function of the blocks in `world/fluid.*`, named after
  `FlowingFluid`'s method; what a tick does with the answers is `server/fluids.cpp`. A question that is answered by a table or
  with a memory (`world::fluid_passes`, the search of `spread`) is held to the method written out the long way in
  `tests/unit/fluid_test.cpp`. What decides whether a block gives way to a fluid, or holds one, is a bit a state
  (`state::kFluidFacts`: a tag and an interface in vanilla) - never a list of blocks in code. A scenario that asks *when*
  freezes the game and steps it (`step_game` in run.py): a fluid is counted in game ticks. New behaviour gets its steps in the
  `water`, `lava`, `waterlogged` or `sponge` section of `tools/vanilla/block_probe.py` - the console's, on a floor of stone, out of
  the bots' reach - and is compared with the vanilla server (`compare.py block_probe -k water -k lava`) before it is called done.
  A class that says otherwise than `SimpleWaterloggedBlock` about taking and giving water is entered in `LIQUID_OVERRIDES`
  (gen_game.py) with what it says, which gen.py turns into a bit of `state::kFluidFacts`.
  A bucket asks the block (`place_liquid`, `pickup_block`: server/fluids.cpp) and aims by a look (`world::clip`, which is
  `BlockGetter.clip` - a new use of it is a `ClipContext` written as its two enums, never a ray of one's own); an item class
  whose `use` is ported is entered in `PORTED_ITEMS` (gen_game.py) with the hooks it overrides. Its cases go into
  `tools/vanilla/swing_probe.py`, which compares the arm, the blocks, the sounds and the slots.
- **Menus**: `server::Menu` is every menu. A new one is a slot table (`at`, `kind_of`), its `quickMoveStack`,
  and - if its container does more than store - a case in `Menu::set` / `remove` / `on_take`; the stacks
  behind it are handed in per operation (`Containers`), never kept. What it shows is compared with vanilla
  by `container_probe.py` before it is called done.
- **What is kept on disk**: a chunk's bytes are `world/chunk_io.*`'s, the game's part of them `server/persist.cpp`'s;
  both begin with a version byte, and a change of layout is a new version that still reads the old. Ids in them
  are this data version's (`world.json` says which). Anything that is read back goes through a reader that
  refuses what it does not know - a stack through `item::read_untrusted`, a section through `Section::load` - and
  a chunk that cannot be read is generated anew and said so in the log, never half taken. New state that should
  survive a restart is written there, marked (`Dimension::mark_dirty`) when it changes, and gets a line in
  `the_world_is_kept` (run.py) - or, when it needs time to pass, a scenario of its own that stops the server and
  starts it again (`falling_block_survives_a_restart`, `a_tick_waits_for_its_chunk`). What an import cannot make
  ours it keeps as a tag (`import.hpp`), never drops. What a player keeps is `server/player_data.*`'s, under the
  same rules (a version byte, a reader that refuses, a file that is whole or new), and gets a line in
  `a_player_comes_back`; no file is read or written on the tick thread - `server::Files` hands it to a worker.
- **Item stacks**: pass them by value (`item::ItemStack`, 8 bytes); a copy of one with components takes a
  reference on its patch, so move where a copy is not meant. Nothing from a client becomes a stack except
  through `item::read_untrusted`, nothing from a tag except through `item::stack_from_nbt`; the server makes
  a patch of its own with `item::PatchBuilder`. A new component gets a decoder in `src/item/components.cpp`
  written from both of its vanilla codecs (StreamCodec = layout, Codec = ranges) with a test of hand-written
  bytes, and its tag form both ways in `src/item/codec.cpp` (the Codec over NbtOps: what a command spells,
  what `/data get` shows) with its cases in `tools/vanilla/component_probe.py` - the probe asks a vanilla
  server for the tag of every stack it hands it. An item class whose use the server implements (beyond
  placing a block) is entered in `PORTED_ITEMS` (gen_game.py) with every hook it overrides, as a block class
  is in `PORTED_BLOCKS`; `ItemInfo::kind` says which it is (`item_kind::kSpawnEgg`), and its `useOn` is a
  case where `Game::on_use_item_on` asks the item, its `use` one in `Game::on_use_item`.
- **NBT**: read with `nbt::Doc` (a tape over the bytes; a `nbt::Tag` is a view that lives as long as the
  Doc and the bytes), write with `nbt::Writer`; no trees of heap nodes, no tags assembled by hand. A tag as
  text is read by `nbt::read_snbt` and nothing else: its functions are the rules of vanilla's grammar, in
  vanilla's order, because the order decides which error a wrong tag gets - a change there goes through the
  wrong tags of `command_probe.py`. A tag that is shown (`Game::save_block_entity`, `save_entity`) is
  written in the order vanilla's code puts its entries: the order they are shown in follows from it. A tag a
  client sent is stored only as `Doc::write` puts it out (one form per value), and a text component only
  through `text::transcode`. Anything that is read by a vanilla `Codec` rather than a `StreamCodec` gets
  its cases into `component_probe.py` before it is called done.
